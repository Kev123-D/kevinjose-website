Logo Design: What Makes a Good Logo

A practical guide to getting a logo that is simple, memorable and works everywhere your business appears.

What a logo is for

A logo is a signature, not an advertisement. Its job is to identify your business quickly and consistently, not to explain everything you do. Some of the most recognisable logos in the world say nothing literal about the product at all. What makes them work is simplicity and years of consistent use.

Qualities of a strong logo

  • Simple: recognisable in a second, even at a glance on a phone.
  • Scalable: clear at 32 pixels as a favicon and at three metres wide on a shopfront.
  • Works in one colour: for embroidery, stamps, engraving and single-colour printing.
  • Distinctive: does not look like your competitors' logos.
  • Appropriate: fits your customer and price point.

Types of logo

  • Wordmark: the name in a custom typeface. Good for short, distinctive names.
  • Symbol or icon: a mark that can stand alone, useful for app icons and social profiles.
  • Combination mark: symbol and name together, with versions that can be used apart. Most small businesses need this.
  • Emblem: name inside a badge or shape. Works well on packaging and merchandise but can be harder to read small.

For Sama Beirut, a butchery brand, the cleaver became the symbol and sits alongside the name and Arabic-inspired lettering, so the van, bags and signage can all use the pieces separately.

How to brief a logo designer

A good brief saves weeks of revisions. Include your one-page brand strategy, three to five words that describe the feeling you want, competitors you want to look different from, where the logo will be used most, and any must-haves such as an Arabic version. Avoid asking for "something modern and clean" without examples; collect a few logos you like and explain why.

Testing before you approve

Before signing off, put the logo on real mock-ups: an Instagram profile, a business card, a bag, a shop sign and a website header. Look at it in black and white and very small. If it falls apart in any of these, it needs simplifying.

Files you should receive

  • Vector files (SVG, AI or EPS) for print and signage
  • PNG files with transparent backgrounds for digital use
  • Full-colour, one-colour black and one-colour white versions
  • Horizontal and stacked layouts, plus the symbol alone
  • The exact colour codes (HEX, RGB, CMYK)

Make sure you own the final files and the rights to use them.

Common logo mistakes

  • Too much detail: fine lines and small text disappear on phones and embroidery.
  • Following trends: a style that is fashionable this year can date quickly. Aim for something that will still look right in ten years.
  • Too many colours: two or three is plenty, and the logo must still work in one.
  • Literal clip-art: a camera for a photographer or a fork for a restaurant rarely feels distinctive.
  • Designing in isolation: a logo chosen without seeing it on real packaging, signage and social media often disappoints in use.

Logos for bilingual businesses in the UAE

Many UAE businesses need their name in both Arabic and English. The two scripts should feel like one family: similar weight, similar height and a shared character. Plan for horizontal and stacked bilingual versions from the start, because adding an Arabic version later is harder to balance and can look like an afterthought on signage.

Where your logo will appear

List every place the logo will actually live before design starts: Instagram and WhatsApp profile pictures, delivery app listings, packaging, uniforms, a shopfront, a vehicle, invoices and the website. Each has different constraints. Social profiles crop logos into circles, delivery apps show them tiny, and embroidery cannot handle gradients. Designing with these in mind avoids expensive fixes later.

Frequently asked questions

How much should a logo cost?

It depends on whether you need just a logo or a full identity with colours, typography and guidelines. A logo on its own is the smaller investment; it pays to budget for the full identity so everything matches.

Should my logo be in Arabic and English?

If you serve both Arabic- and English-speaking customers in the UAE, a bilingual version is worth considering, designed so both scripts feel balanced.

Can I redesign an existing logo?

Yes. A refresh can modernise your logo while keeping the recognition you have built.
