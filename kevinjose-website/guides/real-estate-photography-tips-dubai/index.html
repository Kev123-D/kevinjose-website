Real Estate Photography Tips for Dubai Listings

How agents, owners and holiday home hosts can get listing photos that make buyers and guests stop scrolling.

Why listing photos matter

Most buyers, tenants and holiday guests in Dubai see a property online long before they see it in person. On the property portals and holiday rental sites your first photo sits in a long list of very similar homes, and it decides whether someone clicks. Clear, bright, honest photos help the right people choose to view your property, and help the wrong ones move on.

Prepare the property first

Most of the quality of a real estate photo is decided before the camera arrives. Ask the owner or tenant to:

  • Clear kitchen counters, bathroom surfaces and tables almost completely.
  • Remove personal items such as family photos, shoes, laundry, bins and cables.
  • Make beds neatly with plain, light bedding and plump the cushions.
  • Turn on every light and replace any blown bulbs so colours match.
  • Open curtains and blinds, clean windows and glass doors.
  • Tidy the balcony, terrace or garden, and switch on pool and garden lights for evening shots.

For tenanted properties, agree the shoot time with the tenant in advance and send them this checklist.

Time the shoot for the light

Dubai light is very strong. The middle of the day can create hard shadows and blown-out windows, while early morning and late afternoon give softer, warmer light. Pick the time based on which way the main rooms and views face. For villas with a pool or garden, and for apartments with skyline or sea views, a dusk or "twilight" shot taken just after sunset can become the strongest image of the set.

The shots every listing needs

  • A strong hero image: the exterior, the view or the main living space, whichever is the property's best feature.
  • Every main room: living, dining, kitchen and each bedroom, shot from a corner to show the full space.
  • Bathrooms: straight and clean, with toilet seats down and towels neat.
  • Views and outdoor space: balcony, terrace, garden, pool and the view from the main windows.
  • Building and community amenities: gym, pool, lobby and nearby parks, where they add value.
  • Details: a few close-ups of finishes, fittings or features that set the property apart.

Camera technique that makes a difference

Professional real estate photos usually use a wide lens without extreme distortion, a tripod at about chest height, and straight vertical lines so walls do not lean. Several exposures are blended so both the room and the view through the window are visible. Colours are corrected so white walls look white, not yellow or blue. These details are what make a listing look professional rather than taken on a phone.

Keep it honest

Edit for light, colour and straight lines, but do not remove permanent features, change the view or make rooms look bigger than they are. Misleading photos waste viewings and damage trust. If you use virtual staging for an empty property, label it clearly in the listing.

Video, walkthroughs and drone

A short walkthrough video or a vertical Reel helps buyers and guests understand the layout and flow of a home far better than photos alone, and works well on Instagram, TikTok and in ads. Drone footage can show a villa's plot, the community and nearby landmarks, but drone flights in the UAE need the correct permits, so work with a licensed operator.

Know the advertising rules

In Dubai, property advertising is regulated by the Dubai Land Department and RERA, and listings and ads generally need a valid advertising permit. Rules and permit systems are updated from time to time, so agents should check the current requirements with their brokerage or the Dubai Land Department before publishing photos and videos.

What to send to the portals

Ask for the edited photos in high resolution for brochures and in web size for portals, in landscape for listings and with some vertical crops for social media. Order them with the hero image first, then follow the natural route through the home. Name the files clearly so they are easy to upload and reuse.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos does a listing need?

It depends on the size of the property, but most listings need enough images to show every main room, the outdoor space and the view, usually somewhere between 15 and 30 for an apartment or villa.

How long does a real estate shoot take?

A typical apartment can be shot in about an hour, while a large villa with outdoor spaces, video and a twilight shot can take half a day.

Can you shoot holiday homes and short-term rentals?

Yes. Holiday homes benefit from a mix of room photos, lifestyle details and short vertical videos for social media and booking sites.
