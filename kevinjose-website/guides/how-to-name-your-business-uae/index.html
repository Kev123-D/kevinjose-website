Naming guide

How to Name Your Business in the UAE

A practical process for finding a business name that is memorable, available and approved, whether you are setting up on the mainland or in a free zone.

Why the name matters

Your name is the first piece of your brand that people hear, and usually the one that lasts longest. Logos, colours and websites can be updated later, but changing a name means new licences, new signage, new domains and asking customers to learn it all over again. It is worth taking the time to get it right.

What makes a good business name

  • Easy to say and spell. If someone hears it once on the phone, can they type it into Google or Instagram?
  • Short enough to use. It has to fit on a sign, a sticker, a profile picture and a delivery app tile.
  • Distinctive. Generic names such as "Dubai Best Cleaning" are hard to remember and hard to protect.
  • Room to grow. Avoid tying yourself to one product or one area if you might expand.
  • Works in both languages. Check how it sounds, reads and translates in Arabic as well as English.

Types of business names

Most names fall into a few types, and each has strengths:

  • Descriptive names say what you do. They are clear but harder to stand out and protect.
  • Founder names use a person's name. They feel personal and trustworthy, but tie the business to one person.
  • Invented names are new words. They are very distinctive and easier to secure, but need more marketing to give them meaning.
  • Evocative names suggest a feeling or idea rather than describing the product, such as a word or place that matches the mood of the brand.

Layla Flowers, for example, pairs an evocative, easy-to-say name with a descriptive word, so it feels personal and still tells you what the business sells.

Step 1: Brainstorm widely

Start with your brand strategy: who your customers are, what you offer and how you want people to feel. Then write a long list of 50 or more options without judging them. Use words linked to your product, your values, places, feelings, other languages and combinations of two words. Only start cutting once you have plenty to choose from.

Step 2: Shortlist and test

Cut the list down to five to ten names. Say each one out loud, write it in a sentence ("I ordered from ___ last night"), and mock it up as a simple logo and an Instagram handle. Share the shortlist with a few people from your target audience, not just friends and family, and ask which ones they remember the next day.

Step 3: Check the trade name rules

In the UAE your business name is registered as a trade name with the licensing authority for your mainland emirate or free zone, such as the Department of Economy and Tourism in Dubai. Each authority publishes its own naming rules, and they commonly cover points like these:

  • Names must not be offensive to public morals or religion.
  • Names of government bodies and some protected terms cannot be used.
  • The name usually has to match the business activity and legal form on your licence.
  • Using a person's name or certain words can have extra conditions.

Rules change, so always check the current guidance on your licensing authority's website or with your business setup consultant before you commit, and reserve the name as early as you can.

Step 4: Check domains, social handles and trademarks

Search for the .com and .ae domains, and the handles on Instagram, TikTok and any other platform you plan to use. Then search the UAE trademark register through the Ministry of Economy, and international registers if you plan to sell abroad, to make sure nobody else already owns a similar mark in your category. A trademark lawyer can do a proper clearance search if the name is important to your plans.

Step 5: Check it in Arabic

If your name is in English, check how it sounds when written in Arabic script and how Arabic speakers pronounce it. If it is in Arabic, check the transliteration in English, because there may be several spellings and customers need to find you in search. Ask a native speaker to confirm the name has no unintended meaning in either language.

After you choose

Once the name is reserved and registered, secure the domain and handles straight away, then move on to the logo and visual identity. Our logo design guide and complete brand building guide explain the next steps.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use English words in a UAE trade name?

Generally yes, but each licensing authority has its own rules on language, transliteration and wording, so check the current rules for your emirate or free zone.

Is a trade name the same as a trademark?

No. A trade name is the registered name on your business licence. A trademark protects your name or logo as a brand and is registered separately with the Ministry of Economy.
