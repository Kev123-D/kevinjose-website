How to Prepare for a Food Photo Shoot

A simple plan and checklist to help your restaurant, café or food brand get the most out of a photography day.

Why preparation matters

A food shoot day goes quickly. Dishes look their best for only a few minutes, the kitchen is often working at the same time, and every dish needs to be cooked, styled and shot before it wilts or cools. The more you plan before the photographer arrives, the more usable images you get from the day.

Decide what the photos are for

Start by listing where the photos will be used: delivery apps, the website, the printed menu, Google, Instagram, ads or packaging. Each use has different needs. Delivery apps need a clean, consistent shot of every menu item. Instagram and ads need lifestyle images and short vertical videos. Menus and packaging may need images with space for text. Knowing this decides the shot list, the angles and the formats.

Write a shot list

Write a list of every dish, drink or product to photograph, and for each one note:

  • The angle, such as overhead, 45 degrees or straight on.
  • Whether it is a clean single-dish shot, a styled lifestyle shot, or both.
  • Landscape, square or vertical, and any video needed.
  • Group shots, such as a full table spread, a sharing platter or a combo meal.

Put the dishes in the order they will be shot, grouping those that use the same set-up or the same station in the kitchen.

Plan the kitchen

Ask your chef to prepare for the shoot like a busy service. Prep ingredients in advance, and have the best-looking produce, garnishes and sauces ready. Make "hero" portions with extra care, slightly smaller than usual so they look generous on the plate without crowding it. For some dishes it helps to cook a second plate, so one can be styled while the other is ready to replace it.

Props and styling

Gather plates, bowls, glasses, cutlery, boards, napkins and surfaces that match your brand and your restaurant's look. Matt surfaces usually photograph better than shiny ones. Have small extras ready: fresh herbs, lemon wedges, spare sauce, a brush and some oil to refresh food, paper towels and cotton buds for clean edges. If you use branded packaging for delivery, have a few clean pieces ready to shoot too.

Choose the time and space

Natural light from a large window is often the most flattering for food. If your venue has a bright spot, plan the shoot around the hours when it gets soft daylight, and avoid harsh direct sun. If there is no good window, a photographer can bring lighting. Most restaurants shoot before opening or on a quiet day, so the team can focus and customers are not disturbed.

People and lifestyle shots

Hands pouring a sauce, a chef plating a dish or friends sharing a table make images feel alive and work well on social media and in ads. Decide in advance who will appear, whether staff or models, and make sure everyone is happy to be photographed and to have the images used in marketing. Plan clean uniforms or outfits that suit the brand.

On the day

  • Start with the most important dishes while everyone is fresh.
  • Send each dish out the moment it is ready, and have the next one in progress.
  • Have one person from your team on set to approve shots and keep the kitchen in sync.
  • Shoot a few short videos as you go: pours, sizzles, cutting and plating.
  • Allow a short break between groups of dishes to reset the set.

After the shoot

Agree in advance how many edited images you will receive, in which formats and by when. Ask for web-sized files for apps and social media and high-resolution files for print. Keep one consistent edit across the whole menu so every listing looks like it came from the same kitchen. Then update your delivery apps, Google Business Profile, website and menus together, so customers see the same images everywhere. Frequently asked questions

How many dishes can be shot in a day?

It depends on how complex the styling is and how quickly the kitchen can prepare dishes. Clean menu shots are much faster than fully styled lifestyle scenes, so agree the shot list with your photographer in advance.

Should I use real food or fake food for photos?

Real food, prepared carefully, looks best and keeps the photos honest. Small styling tricks such as a little oil for shine are fine, but the dish should look like what customers receive.

Can you also shoot video for Reels and TikTok?

Yes. I usually shoot short vertical videos alongside the photos, so you get content for social media and ads from the same day.
