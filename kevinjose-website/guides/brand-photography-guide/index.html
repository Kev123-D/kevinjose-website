Brand Photography: How to Plan a Shoot

Your photos do the selling before customers read a word. Here is how to plan a brand shoot that looks like your business, not stock.

What brand photography is

Brand photography is a planned set of images that show your products, your space, your people and the feeling of your brand in a consistent style. Unlike stock photos, which competitors can use too, brand photography is yours alone and instantly recognisable when it is done consistently.

The types of photo you need

  • Product shots: clean, accurate images for your website, menus and online store.
  • Lifestyle shots: products in use, in real settings, that help customers picture them in their own lives.
  • Detail shots: textures, packaging, ingredients and craftsmanship.
  • Team and founder portraits: people buy from people, especially small businesses.
  • Space and location: your shop, restaurant, studio or property.

Start with a mood board

Collect 15 to 20 images that capture the light, colours, styling and composition you want. Make sure they match your brand colours and the feeling in your strategy. Soft morning light and neutral props suit a calm, premium brand; bold colour and hard shadows suit a playful, modern one.

Write a shot list

List every image you need, where it will be used and in what shape: vertical for Instagram Stories and TikTok, square for feeds, wide for website banners. Planning shapes in advance stops you from cropping the most important part of an image later.

Shoot once, use everywhere

A well-planned shoot can supply your website, social media, menus, packaging and paid ads for months. For Layla Flowers, the same shoot produced the website hero, product photos for the online store and the creative for Meta carousel ads.

Keep it consistent

Use the same light, editing style and colour treatment across every shoot, and record it in your brand guidelines. Consistency is what makes a feed look like a brand rather than a random collection of photos.

Planning the shoot day

  • Location: confirm access, permissions and the best time of day for light.
  • Products and props: prepare more than you need, cleaned and ready.
  • People: brief models or staff on clothing colours that suit the brand palette.
  • Schedule: shoot products that wilt or melt, like flowers and food, first.
  • Formats: capture vertical, square and wide versions of key shots.

Photography for different industries

Restaurants and food brands need appetising close-ups, menu shots and the atmosphere of the space. Florists and gifting brands need clean product shots for online ordering plus lifestyle images showing the gift being given. Real estate and hospitality need bright, straight interiors and views. Retail and fashion need product detail, styled flat lays and people wearing or using the product. Planning for your industry's specific uses makes the shoot far more valuable.

Using your photos well

Name and organise your files by product and use, keep the full-resolution originals, and describe each image accurately when you upload it to your website, using alt text that explains what is shown. That helps search engines understand your images, so your work can appear in image search for the products and places you photograph.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I do a brand shoot?

Most businesses benefit from one main shoot when they launch or rebrand, then smaller shoots for new products, seasons or campaigns.

Can I use my phone instead?

For quick social content, yes. For your website, packaging and ads, professional photography gives more consistent quality and lighting.

Where should a brand shoot happen?

Wherever tells your story best: your shop, kitchen or property, a studio for clean product shots, or a location in Dubai that matches your mood.
